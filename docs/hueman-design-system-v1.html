00 · Product context

What we are building, and what that demands of the interface

hueman.studio is a trainable voice and visual core for personal brands. It learns how someone sounds and how they look, then helps them create, check and ship content that stays on-brand. hue is their distinct colour and positioning. human is the promise that the output sounds like them, not like AI slop.

Who is paying

The payable middle of the creator and personal-brand market: coaches, consultants, B2B founders and solopreneurs. People who take their brand seriously enough to spend 29 to 99 dollars a month, but cannot justify a 1,000 to 3,000 dollar a month agency.

What is true of the userWhat the interface has to do about it
They are the brand. The output goes out under their own name.Never ship generated text without showing how confident the system is and what it based that on. Trust is the product; a confident wrong answer costs a customer.
They are not designers or engineers. No one is trained on this tool.Conventional patterns everywhere. This is not the place for an invented interaction. Novelty belongs in the visual treatment, never in how a control behaves.
They are paying 29 to 99 a month out of their own pocket, monthly.The product is re-evaluated every thirty days. Perceived quality is retention. Loading states, empty states and error copy are not polish here, they are the churn surface.
They write at night, on a phone, between calls.Dark mode ships in v1. Every control clears 44px. The composer works at 360px wide.
Their competitor is a blank page and a generic AI chat window.The interface has to look like it belongs to them. The spectrum is how a customer's own hue enters the product surface, which is why it is a first-class token tier and not decoration.

Validated pains

Four recurring complaints from the team's own market research across public creator and founder forums. Treat this as directional signal about what people say out loud, not as quantified research: none of it is a controlled study, and the counts below are impressions of frequency rather than measurements. It earns its place here because two of the four change what gets built.

PainSignalWhat it changes in the build
1. Consistency
"What do I even post?"		 The broadest of the four. The phrase "struggle to post consistently" recurs across a dozen unrelated forums. Create must never open on a blank composer. The blank page is the pain, so handing the user a cursor and a placeholder hands the pain back. The empty state in section 12 now proposes topics drawn from their own core instead of asking for one.
2. "It sounds like AI"
Not like me		 The sharpest emotional wedge. An entire anxiety culture around it: people scraping tens of thousands of posts to detect AI writing, and hunting prompts that will "write like a human". Already load-bearing across the system. It is why the confidence number is displayed wherever a draft is generated (15.1), why every drift finding cites evidence from the user's own samples rather than a generic rule (15.4), and why section 16 forbids the interface from ever saying "this sounds like AI".
3. Clarity and positioning
The message keeps changing		 Recurring theme: personal brands fail even with good content, because the message keeps changing. People follow clarity. An explicit non-goal. See below. This one is real and it is not tool-solvable, so nothing in the interface should imply the product solves it.
4. ROI doubt
"Wealth, or a digital mirage?"		 Surfaces as scepticism before purchase and as churn after it. At a monthly price the question gets asked again every thirty days. A gap. Nothing in this document currently answers it. The product has no surface that shows accumulated value. See the open questions in section 18.
Non-goal: the product does not decide anyone's positioning

Pain 3 is the deepest of the four and the product cannot fix it. Positioning is a methodology problem that gets solved by thinking, usually with a human, not by software holding a style guide. hueman.studio holds a voice and checks against it. It does not tell anyone what to stand for.

Concretely, for engineering: do not build a positioning wizard, a "find your niche" quiz, or an onboarding step that asks the user to declare their one big idea before they can proceed. Every one of those promises something the product cannot deliver, and the gap between the promise and the output is where trust dies. Where positioning genuinely needs work, the honest move is to say so and point at help, not to generate a positioning statement.

The three surfaces

Why assembly rather than delivery

A tool that hands over a finished draft produces something the user then edits. The output stays ours and they approved it. A tool that hands over parts and an order of operations produces something they made. The difference is not tone, it is authorship, and authorship is the thing this market is actually short of.

This is a documented effect rather than a hunch. In the study that named it, people who assembled an IKEA box valued it at $1.46. People given the same box and the same task who did not finish valued it at $0.59. Participants who built a set and then took it apart again showed no premium at all.

01 · Using this document

How to read this, and what counts as done

Everything with a -- token name or a .hu- class is normative: build it exactly. Everything prefixed .d- is this document's own chrome and ships nowhere.

The token contract

Three tiers, and the rule that makes them worth having: a component may only reference tier 2. If you find yourself typing a hex value or a --grey- token inside a component rule, the semantic tier is missing a token. Add it there instead.

TierExampleWho may reference it
1. Primitive--grey-400: #8C8C8CTier 2 only. Never a component, never a page.
2. Semantic--border-default: var(--grey-400)Every component. This is the layer that flips for dark mode.
3. Component.hu-btn--primaryApplication code.
Why this matters here

v0 of the kit had only tier 1. That is why --hue-coral meant both "the coral in the spectrum" and "the error colour", and why three badge colours ended up hardcoded with no token at all. Dark mode is impossible to add cleanly on top of that, and dark mode ships in v1.

Definition of done, per component

  • All six states exist. Default, hover, focus-visible, active, disabled, and loading where the component can be in flight.
  • Keyboard map implemented exactly as the component's table specifies, including Escape and arrow keys where listed.
  • ARIA role and state attributes present and updating, not just on first render.
  • Renders correctly in both themes with no colour declared outside the token tiers.
  • Every interactive target is at least 44 by 44, including targets inside the component such as remove and close buttons.
  • Layout does not shift between loading, error and loaded states.
  • Works at 360px wide without horizontal page scroll.
02 · Principles

Three laws, written as rules you can fail a review against

Three, not thirty. A principles page listing fifteen laws gives every one of them equal weight, which is the same as giving none of them any. Each of these three changed something concrete in v1.

1. Fitts's Law: the target is the whole thing, not the glyph

Time to hit a target falls as the target grows. In v0 the default button computed to 43px, the small button to 33px, the mono CTA link to roughly 17px, the nav links to roughly 16px, and the checkbox to 18px. All below the 44px floor.

RuleHow it is enforced
Every interactive element is at least 44 by 44 CSS px.--tap-min: 44px and --control-h-sm: 44px. Applied as min-height, never as fixed height, so text can wrap.
One exception: a link inside a sentence.Inline links inherit the line box and cannot be padded without breaking the paragraph. They are exempt, and only they. Everything else, including every close and remove button, meets the floor.
A control that must look small still hits 44.Use the .hu-hit expansion: a transparent 44px pseudo-element centred on the control. Visual size unchanged, target size correct.
A label is part of its control's target.Every field has matching for and id. Checkbox and radio rows are wrapped in the label element, which is what turns an 18px box into a full-width row.
Adjacent targets have at least 8px between them.gap: var(--space-2) minimum on any row of controls.
Destructive actions are not adjacent to their most likely neighbour.Delete sits at the bottom of a menu behind a separator, never beside Save.

2. Feedback, with the Doherty Threshold: nothing happens silently

Under 100ms reads as instant. Past 400ms with nothing shown, the user stops believing the click registered. Past 1,000ms they lose the thread. This matters more here than in most products, because generation is slow by nature and the user is watching something they will put their name on.

ElapsedRequired responseComponent
0 to 400msNothing. Do not flash a spinner, it reads as slower than silence.None. Use the spinner's delay prop.
400ms to 1sState inside the control itself. Dimensions held.Button isLoading
1s to 10sSkeleton of the shape that is coming, or determinate progress if the total is known.Skeleton, Progress Bar
Over 10sAssume they left the tab. Named steps with an estimate, and a result that survives a refresh.Stepper, Progress Bar, Toast on completion
On finishSay what happened, in the past tense, where the action was taken.Toast for transient, Alert for anything the user must act on

3. Von Restorff Effect: one primary action per view

The thing that differs from its neighbours is the thing that gets acted on. v0 put four button treatments plus a link CTA on one row at near-equal weight, and made the primary button turn accent blue on hover, so the two heaviest treatments traded identities mid-interaction.

Do

One filled button per view. Black is the primary. Accent blue is reserved for the single conversion action, typically upgrade or publish. Everything else is outline or ghost.

Don't

Two filled buttons side by side, or a hover state that changes a button's variant. Hover changes intensity, never identity: --action-primary-bg-hover is a lightened black, not the accent.

Convention versus distinction

hueman's visual identity is distinctive on purpose. Its interaction patterns are not. Novelty is permitted in visual treatment and forbidden in interaction pattern, unless there is a stated reason and a test behind it. A coach who has never used a brand tool should be able to operate this on the strength of every other app they have used.

Typography

GT America carries the interface. GT Super Display Light is the editorial counterpoint and appears in hero statements only. GT America Mono carries labels, tokens, metadata and anything numeric.

Licensing: read before you build

GT America and GT Super are licensed from Grilli Type and are not embedded in this file, so what you are reading now renders in the documented fallbacks. Grilli Type prices a web licence on projected unique monthly visitors as a one-time fee, not a subscription, and one licence covers any number of sites and domains controlled by the same organisation.

Two consequences for engineering. Grilli Type only offers self-hosted webfonts, so there is no CDN to point at: the TTF, WOFF and WOFF2 files ship from our own origin. And the free trial fonts may be used in mockups and prototypes only, never in production, so the licence has to land before launch.

Motion and elevation

v0 hardcoded 0.18s and 0.2s across eight rules, translated three components on hover, and had no reduced-motion block anywhere. Motion is now tokenised and every animation is behind a preference query.

TokenValueUse
--motion-instant80msColour change on hover. Fast enough to feel like a property, not an animation.
--motion-fast120msButtons, inputs, links, tabs. The default for anything the pointer is on.
--motion-base180msSwitch throw, menu open, card elevation.
--motion-slow280msModal and drawer entry, progress fill, meter fill.
--ease-standardcubic-bezier(.2,0,0,1)Everything that starts and ends on screen.
--ease-entercubic-bezier(0,0,0,1)Things arriving. Decelerate in.
--ease-exitcubic-bezier(.3,0,1,1)Things leaving. Accelerate out, and always faster than entry.
Non-negotiable

The global prefers-reduced-motion block is in the base layer and collapses all durations to 0.01ms. Components with continuous animation, the spinner and the skeleton shimmer, additionally provide a static alternative rather than simply freezing mid-cycle. Nothing in this product may flash more than three times per second.

Elevation order

A named scale, so that the first time a modal, a dropdown and a toast are open at once nobody has to invent a number. Elevation is a stacking contract, not a shadow palette.

Interaction states

v0 documented one state: hover. This is the universal matrix. Any component that can be in a state below must implement it, and the visual treatment is the same everywhere so users only learn it once.

Disabled versus read-only versus hidden

A disabled control is one the user could enable. A read-only control is one they will never edit but should still see. A control they cannot ever use is not disabled, it is absent. Do not disable a plan upgrade button when the user is on the top plan; remove it and say what plan they are on.

Focus management is the whole job here. Every overlay opens from a trigger, and every overlay returns focus to that trigger when it closes.

Modal

Retraining rebuilds the core from all 11 samples. Drafts already written keep their scores; new drafts use the new core. This takes about two minutes and cannot be undone.

Props isOpen (53%), aria-label (33%), size (27%). Required role="dialog", aria-modal="true" and aria-labelledby pointing at the title. Do

Name the consequence in the title and put the outcome in the confirm button: "Retrain core", not "OK".

Don't

Use a modal for anything the user can carry on working around. Blocking the whole screen for a non-blocking message is what trains people to dismiss dialogs unread.

UX copy

The product's promise is that it does not sound like AI slop. An interface that says "Oops! Something went wrong 🎉" has broken that promise before the user has read a single generated word. Interface copy is part of the demo.

17 · Shipping

Acceptance criteria

A component is not done when it looks right. Run this before opening the pull request; it is the same list QA will run.

Keyboard

  • Every interactive element is reachable by Tab in the order it appears visually.
  • Focus is visible on every one of them. 2px ring, 2px offset, and never removed by a reset.
  • Escape closes every modal, menu, popover, tooltip and combobox, and focus returns to the trigger.
  • Arrow keys work in menus, listboxes, radio groups, sliders, spinbuttons, steppers and pagination.
  • Radio groups and menus are one tab stop, not one per item, via roving tabindex.
  • No keyboard trap anywhere except inside an open modal, where the trap is intentional.

Screen reader

  • Every form control has a programmatic name via a wired label, and clicking the label focuses it.
  • Errors are announced via aria-invalid plus aria-describedby pointing at the message.
  • Loading is announced via aria-busy on controls, role="status" on spinners and skeletons.
  • Alerts and toasts announce immediately via role="alert" or aria-live="assertive".
  • Icon-only controls have an aria-label that says the action, not the icon.
  • Decorative graphics are hidden with aria-hidden="true" or alt="".

Visual and layout

  • Text contrast is at least 4.5:1, and 3:1 for large text, borders, icons and focus rings.
  • No state is signalled by colour alone. Every one carries an icon, a dot, a label or a position.
  • Both themes render correctly, with no colour declared outside the token tiers.
  • Every target is at least 44 by 44, with at least 8px between adjacent targets. Links inside a sentence are the only exception; icon-only and close controls use .hu-hit to reach it.
  • Nothing reflows between default, loading, error and loaded.
  • Works at 360px with no horizontal page scroll; wide tables scroll inside their own container.
  • Respects prefers-reduced-motion, and nothing flashes more than three times a second.
  • Zooms to 200% without content being cut off or overlapping.

Behaviour

  • Nothing over 400ms happens silently. See the threshold table in section 02.
  • No double-submit. The control disables itself for the duration of the request.
  • One primary action per view.
  • Destructive actions are confirmed and the confirm button names the outcome.
  • Every list has an empty state and it names the next action.
  • Every request has an error state that says what to do next.

The one lint rule worth adding

Fail the build on any hex value, rgb(), or --grey-* / --hue-* reference inside components/ or patterns/. That single rule is what keeps the token architecture from eroding in month three, and it is what makes the next theme a one-file change.

Open questions for the team

  • Font licence. Grilli Type prices on projected unique monthly visitors as a one-time fee. Someone needs to give them a traffic projection and get the licence in place before launch, since the trial files are prototype-only.
  • Score bands. The 85 and 60 thresholds in 15.3 are a starting point, not a finding. They should be set from real check data once there is some, and the band names may need to change with them.
  • Proof of value has no surface. ROI doubt is one of the four validated pains and nothing in v1.0 answers it. The Studio plan already promises a weekly brand report, which is the obvious hook: a view showing score trend over time, drafts shipped, drift caught before publishing, and time between first draft and publish. It needs a decision on what is actually worth counting before a pattern is designed, because counting the wrong thing here reads as vanity metrics and makes the doubt worse.
  • Topic suggestion needs an owner. The Create empty state now proposes topics rather than requesting one, which assumes the core can produce them. Whether those come from the ingested samples, from what the user has published recently, or from something else is a product and model decision, not a UI one, and the pattern in section 12 is a placeholder until it is made.
  • Not assessable from a spec. Whether users abandon during training, whether the confidence number reassures or alarms, and whether the drift findings read as helpful or as criticism. Each of those needs a test on real users; the document can specify the test but cannot report its result.
